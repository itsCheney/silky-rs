# 同級生 Remake

English | [简体中文](doukyuusei.zh-CN.md)

Steam release (Shiravune, 2022) in English, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese; there is no Japanese text. The runtime knows the game by `doukyuusei.exe` in its folder; the build checked is `doukyuusei.exe` 1.0.4.0 with SHA-256 `9c9841fbca3a422a36f7ddef696f6164b4206ff622ecdb5cc1b4f45c8b231451`, linked 2022-03-15. Its engine profile is [engine-2022](../builds/engine-2022.md). The screen is 1920 x 1080.

The game is an adventure in a town: the hero spends the days of a summer break going from place to place, and the story scenes start from what he looks at and whom he meets. The scripts run that part themselves and ask the engine for the screens (the room, the town map, the time of day, the people present).

## What works

- Text tables: each script `X.MES` has `X_EN.MAP`, `X_CN.MAP` and `X_TW.MAP`. `Game.24(id, 0)` takes line `id` of the running script's table for the next `MesWnd.3`; `Game.24(id, 1)` names the speaker from `NAMES_<lang>.MAP`; `Game.24(name, 2)` loads the table the script names: the program adds `_EN.MAP`, `_CN.MAP` or `_TW.MAP` to the argument as it stands and keeps that table until the next call, whatever script runs. It does not take the name from the running script's file: the update archive's variants of a script (`MISA_Z_R18.MES`, and the other `*_Z_R18.MES`) pass the plain name (`misa_z`) themselves, and have no tables of their own. `MISA_Z_EN.MAP` has 334 lines, the number of messages of `MISA_Z_R18.MES`; the plain `MISA_Z.MES` has 205 and uses the first ones. In the lines, `\n` breaks the line, `\i` turns italics on and off and `:myname:` is the hero's name (Takurou, 拓郎). `Game.22` makes the hero the speaker.
- The message window, measured under Wine and read from the executable:

  - Name plate: rows 1350..1400 of the sheet (a star and a gold rule) at (231, 799), the name from (294, 800).
  - Text in the game's bitmap fonts from (242, 870): half-width letters take a 20-pixel cell, full-width characters two; lines are 48 pixels apart. Each glyph is drawn black at its eight neighbours, then white, as the executable does. `\i` switches to `font_han_i.akb`.
  - Word wrap as in the executable: a word (with the space after it) goes to the next line when its letters would reach 74 cells from the line start; its trailing space does not count. Checked on many English lines under Wine.
  - Buttons from `mes_o.area`: skip, skip all, auto, quick save and quick load along the bottom (ids 0..4, screen pixels) and the voice and close buttons (ids 5 and 6, relative to the window top; drawn a pixel left of their areas). Cells of `message_p.akb` with rows by state.
  - Click wait: the yellow mark of `message_p.akb` (three 48-pixel frames down from (1716, 700)) at (1723, 996). The frame time is a guess.
  - The first lines match the original under Wine within a few levels per pixel.

- The first-start intro (`Game.31()`, no values; modal), read from the executable and compared with a Wine run (layering and pace) The main script calls it once, when byte 8014 is 0 (it sets it at once), after the short title screen of `title_s_bg.akb` and before the title screen proper. Two picture objects on layer 0, the petal (`hana_0000.akb`, 128 x 128) and the language's `title_n_bg_<lang>.akb`; the petal is drawn over the picture, as Wine shows it (the layer getter is the same for both classes and the engine list is a stable ascending sort, so the executable's order of making does not settle it). First wait, 320 frames: the petal starts at (-200, -200) turned 60 degrees at 1.5 times its size; steps 1 to 319 place its top left corner on a uniform cubic B-spline through 17 waypoints (parameter -1 at step 1, plus 0.053125 a step, the end waypoints counted twice: (375, -128) down the left edge to (-6, 329) and on along the bottom to (1294, 816)) and, from step 20, turn it from 60 to -110 degrees and shrink it from 1.5 to 0.5 times, with a flip about its axis (scale across to 0 and back, 10 frames each) in several windows; the turn and the scale are about the middle of the petal and the turn is kept in whole degrees. A table of the turn and scales per step is in the code. Second wait, 64 frames: the petal's opacity goes from 255 to 0 (`255 - 255 n / 64`, whole steps, taken away at the end) and the colour picture, from no opacity, is wiped in through the rule `62.bin`: a threshold rises 512 / 63 a step and a pixel with mask value r takes the opacity threshold - r once the threshold has passed it (the original skips pixels already past the 264-wide ramp, as here). Then the colour picture is drawn into the background for good, over the whole screen, and the script goes on (it then loads the real title). Each wait ends one pass after its last step, so 386 passes in all. A pass is one tick of the original's timer thread: a set is ticked when more than 15 ms have passed since its last tick (`timeGetTime`, 1 ms timer period), the thread then draws and presents once and sleeps until the next due time; there is no catching up, so a pass is 16 ms or the time to draw, whichever is longer. Here a pass is a sixtieth of a second. Under Wine in a virtual display the draw is slow (about 40 ms a pass: the petal about 14 s, the wipe 2 to 3 s), which is why it runs about 2.6 times slower there; the same holds for every wait of the engine. The wait reads no click, so a click does not skip it (the original's wait loop has no skip key; here the skip mode does not either). Plays no sound. Still to check: whether a click skips it, and that the colour picture is what stays for the title.

- The calendar (`Game.25`): the executable writes a year into the raw area from byte 18000, 32 bytes a month, a byte a day: the weekday (1 Monday to 7 Sunday), plus 8 on a holiday, 0 past the month's end. The scripts count a month's days and read the weekdays from it.
- The walking maps (`Game.20`, the program's `CCps`), read from the executable. The program takes a call's values from the last one back; the sub command is the last.
  - Sub 0 (`name.mpx`) loads the grid: width and height (u16), then 5 bytes a 48-pixel tile, row by row; byte 4 is the wall flag.
  - Sub 4 (`name.ccd`) loads the walkers. Header words: walker table, program table (a u16 offset per program), start table (camera x, y, walker x, y, a byte each) and tile patterns; there are (word 1 − 9) / 14 walkers of 14 bytes: flags (1 shown, 0x10 stopped by walls, 0x20 the camera follows, 0x40 the hero), look, width, height, x and y (u16), facing × 16 + frame, program, command, count and program counter. Sub 1 places a walker from the start table, sub 5 gives it a program, sub 7 sets its flags, sub 14 and 22 step and turn it, sub 20 makes it follow another, sub 31 puts the walkers and camera back from the raw area (camera at bytes 7097 and 7098, walker i at 7091 + 3i).
  - Programs: 0 restarts, 1 and 8 face, 2 to 5 step up, down, left, right, 6 follows the trail, 9 waits, 14 takes the player's input.
  - Sub 12 (`mode`, `name.eve`) loads the places: 12-byte records of id, left, top, right, bottom and the facings they take (up 1, down 2, left 4, right 8), up to 0xffff. A walker of size w × h at (x, y) is in a place when x ≤ right, x + w > left, y + 1 ≤ bottom, y + h > top and its facing is allowed. Sub 13 returns the place the hero is in.
  - Sub 30 lets the player walk and returns the place reached. Arrow keys step a tile, Shift or Ctrl two; holding the mouse walks towards the pointer in eight directions. The hero is stopped by walls along the rows y + 1 to y + h − 1 and slides along them as the program does. A step takes 66, 33 or 16 ms as the map speed setting says (`/Config/MapSpeed`: slow, normal, fast; normal is 33 ms).
  - Sub 15 `(walker, x, y)` walks a walker to a target (the map jump uses it for the hero): it searches from the target towards the walker, greedily by the side the walker lies on and backing out of dead ends (the walker is taken as 3 × 2 tiles), then takes the lowest stamp seen along any straight line from each tile, which gives a trail of directions per tile; it is not the shortest way. Four script bytes (1691, 1693, 1694, 4068) change the order of the tries. When a trail is found the walker's command becomes "follow the trail" with its wall flag cleared; each tick (sub 9) it takes the step the trail gives under its feet, a diagonal as a vertical and a horizontal step. Sub 17 says whether the walk goes on (written to g18 like sub 13's result, 0xffff or 0); sub 16 ends it and gives the walker back its flags, command and count, so it goes on with its program. Sub 26 waits one step time (66, 33 or 16 ms by the map speed) between ticks. Checked against a port of the search on all 1260 pairs of destinations of the town map. A click, Escape or Delete during the walk ends it at its next step when byte 4067 is set: that step reports finished and the walker is given back as on arrival (sub 16), and the click is no map click.
