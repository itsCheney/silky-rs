# 同級生 Remake

English | [简体中文](doukyuusei.zh-CN.md)

Steam release (Shiravune, 2022) in English, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese; there is no Japanese text. The runtime knows the game by `doukyuusei.exe` in its folder; the build described here is `doukyuusei.exe` 1.0.4.0, SHA-256 `9c9841fbca3a422a36f7ddef696f6164b4206ff622ecdb5cc1b4f45c8b231451`, linked 2022-03-15. Engine profile: [engine-2022](../builds/engine-2022.md). The screen is 1920 x 1080.

The game is an adventure in a town: the hero spends the days of a summer break going from place to place, and story scenes start from what he looks at and whom he meets. The scripts run that part themselves and ask the engine for the screens (the room, the town map, the time of day, the people present).

Headless, `silky-run snap` plays the opening, looks around the hero's room, sleeps an hour, then walks the town to one place after another (the shortest way, without walking through other places), looking at and talking to everything inside; it runs through more than two hundred visits over several days.

## What works

### Text and messages

- Text tables: each script `X.MES` has `X_EN.MAP`, `X_CN.MAP`, `X_TW.MAP`.
  - `Game.24(id, 0)` takes line `id` of the running script's table for the next `MesWnd.3`; `(id, 1)` names the speaker from `NAMES_<lang>.MAP`; `(name, 2)` loads the table the script names. The program adds `_EN.MAP`, `_CN.MAP` or `_TW.MAP` to the argument as it stands and keeps that table until the next call, whatever script runs.
  - The table name is not taken from the running script's file. The update archive's script variants (`MISA_Z_R18.MES` and the other `*_Z_R18.MES`) pass the plain name (`misa_z`) themselves and have no tables of their own. `MISA_Z_EN.MAP` has 334 lines, the message count of `MISA_Z_R18.MES`; the plain `MISA_Z.MES` has 205 and uses the first ones.
  - In lines, `\n` breaks the line, `\i` toggles italics, `:myname:` is the hero's name (Takurou, 拓郎). `Game.22` makes the hero the speaker.
- Message window:
  - Starts at y = 730 (`/Config/MesWnd/PosY`): rows 0..350 of `message_p.akb`, a black band at 63 % opacity (default `Alpha` 37), then rows 350..700, the frame lines. The band's alpha masks the settings' window colour at their opacity.
  - Name plate: rows 1350..1400 of the sheet (a star and a gold rule) at (231, 799); name from (294, 800).
  - Text in the game's bitmap fonts from (242, 870): half-width letters take a 20-pixel cell, full-width characters two; lines 48 apart. Each glyph is drawn black at its eight neighbours, then white. `\i` switches to `font_han_i.akb`.
  - Word wrap: a word (with the space after it) goes to the next line when its letters would reach 74 cells from the line start; the trailing space does not count.
  - Buttons from `mes_o.area`: skip, skip all, auto, quick save, quick load along the bottom (ids 0..4, screen pixels); voice and close (ids 5 and 6, relative to the window top, drawn a pixel left of their areas). Cells of `message_p.akb`, rows by state.
  - Click wait: the yellow mark of `message_p.akb` (three 48-pixel frames down from (1716, 700)) at (1723, 996). Frame time is a guess.
- Chinese text: 40 pixels a character, 37 to a line. A full-width character missing from `font_zen` (e.g. 拓) still draws in the message text from the TrueType face the frontend passes (`--font`), 34 pixels tall with the 8-direction black outline, in a 40 x 40 cell; the executable has no such fallback there (its blit reads outside the sheet).
  - The hero's name on the name plate (and in the backlog) is drawn whole with the GDI font, 40 high, when any of its characters is wide (East Asian Width), the pen moving 40 for a wide character and 20 for a narrow one. A name of narrow characters, and every other speaker's name, uses the bitmap font. The test is the width, not a missing glyph.
  - GDI text (below, and the hint bars, flag list, save card and comment field, name entry) never uses the bitmap font. The executable asks GDI for SimHei (name entry: MS Gothic) at a cell height `H`, with three fonts: weight 400 at `H`, 700 at `H` - 2, 900 at `H` - 4; the flags pick one (bit 0 gives 700, else bit 4 gives 900, else 400); the game's style is 3, the 700 font with an outline. Outline: the glyph in black at nine offsets (0 to 2 across and down), then the glyph in white one pixel in; shadow and plain passes exist but only the plain one is used (the comment field). A glyph's gray bitmap (65 levels) becomes alpha `level x 255 / 64`, its top lies at the font's ascent minus the glyph's top above the baseline below the cell's top. A wide character (East Asian Width: full-width, wide and ambiguous) moves the pen a whole size, a narrow one half, narrow glyphs sitting at the pen. Here the face is the frontend's TrueType face standing in for SimHei and MS Gothic, scaled so that its ascent plus descent is the cell height and placed by its ascent; weights 700 and 900 are made by widening the glyph sideways by 1 and 2 pixels (an approximation of the heavier faces). Without a face these characters are not drawn.
- Backgrounds take the common `Game.12` calls.
- Movies: `Movie.0` (file), `Movie.3` plays and returns whether it played, `Movie.2` closes, as in the 2017 and 2021 builds; the opening plays after the first phone call.
- Calendar (`Game.25`): the executable writes a year into the raw area from byte 18000, 32 bytes a month, a byte a day: weekday (1 Monday to 7 Sunday), plus 8 on a holiday, 0 past the month's end. Scripts count a month's days and read weekdays from it.
- Small commands:
  - `Game.23(name)` has no sub command (its one string value counts as sub -1) and returns the picture name in the game's language, `x.akb` to `x_en.akb`, `x_ch.akb` or `x_tw.akb`, or the name itself if that file does not exist.
  - `Game.10(11)` brings the message window back and takes the status plate down unless byte 4084 is set (the window itself is shown whenever a line is up).
  - `Game.10(n, 15)` shows the speaker's icon n (from 1; `message_icon.akb`, 144 x 144 cells, four to a row) at (78, 848); `Game.10(16)` takes it away. `Game.10` values below 10 do nothing.
  - `Game.10(10)` (no values) wipes the text drawn on the message window; the window itself is not touched. Here the line's text is emptied while the window stays up if it was (library function 47, a scene reset, calls it).
- Pan over a tall picture (`Game.33`; `E09.MES` and `KAORI_X.MES`; nothing returned, the first value counts as the sub command):
  - The script loads a 1920 x 2160 picture into sprite layer 1 (`Sprite.1(1, "e09.akb")`) and pushes, in order: step in pixels (4 in E09, 16 in KAORI_X), delay of a step in ms (15), end y (0), end x (0), start y (1080), start x (0). Each step copies the 1920 x 1080 window of layer 1 at the current position onto the screen, moves the position on by distance / steps and waits the delay; the steps number the longer distance divided by the step, rounded down (E09 270, about 4 s; KAORI_X 67, about 1 s). It ends with a copy at exactly the end position.
  - The screen speed setting, when not the first place, makes the step four times larger (a quarter of the steps). The wait can be cut short like the others (skip). The original also does nothing at all when the "skip scenes" setting is on; that setting is not in the runtime.
  - Before it E09 copies the window at y 1080 of layer 1 onto the screen with `Sprite.3` (nine values, pushed in this order: flag, source sprite, source y, source x, target sprite, height, width, target y, target x), so the scene opens on the lower half and the pan goes up to the top. The order is read from the scripts' values (the window y 1080 matches the pan's start) and not from the engine's sprite code. Here that call sets which window of the layer's picture is drawn (a copy onto sprite 0 at (0, 0) of a layer that exists); the pan then draws its own windows, and the last one stays until the layer is loaded again. Code in `pan.rs`.

### Title, intro and name entry

- Title menu (`Game.28`): `title_bg_<lang>.akb` (logo included) and six entries from `title.area`, 494 x 78 cells of `title_p_<lang>.akb` three to a row with four state rows; continue and quick load are grey without saves. Extras page (`title2.area`): album, memory reel, music, a hidden fourth entry, back. New game returns 0, the extras 257 to 260; quit closes the game.
- First-start intro (`Game.31()`, modal): the main script calls it once when byte 8014 is 0 (it sets it at once), after the short title screen of `title_s_bg.akb` and before the title screen proper. No sound; clicks and keys do not skip it.
  - Two picture objects on layer 0: the petal (`hana_0000.akb`, 128 x 128, drawn over) and `title_n_bg_<lang>.akb`.
  - First wait, 320 frames: the petal starts at (-200, -200), turned 60 degrees, at 1.5 times its size. Steps 1 to 319 put its top left corner on a uniform cubic B-spline through 17 waypoints (parameter -1 at step 1, +0.053125 a step, end waypoints counted twice: (375, -128) down the left edge to (-6, 329) and along the bottom to (1294, 816)). From step 20 it turns from 60 to -110 degrees (whole degrees) and shrinks from 1.5 to 0.5 times, with a flip about its axis (scale across to 0 and back, 10 frames each) in several windows; turn and scale are about the petal's middle.
  - Second wait, 64 frames: petal opacity `255 - 255 n / 64` (whole steps, removed at the end) while the colour picture is wiped in from no opacity through rule `62.bin`: a threshold rises 512 / 63 a step and a pixel with mask value r takes opacity threshold - r once the threshold has passed it (pixels already past the 264-wide ramp are skipped). Then the colour picture is drawn into the background over the whole screen and the script goes on to the real title.
  - Each wait ends one pass after its last step: 386 passes. A pass is one tick of the original's timer thread (a set ticks when more than 15 ms have passed since its last tick, then draws and presents once; no catching up). Here a pass is a sixtieth of a second, as for every engine wait.
- Name entry (`Game.18`, no values, returns none; new game runs it once). The page shows the script's background even while the screen is covered (scripts ask with `Screen.4`); the cover is still there when it closes; here it is lifted when the page opens.
  - Over the script's `keyboard_bg.akb` the page draws `keyboard_p_<lang>.akb`: gray "Direct Input" bar, dark "Name" plate, left/right arrows and Backspace (188 x 58, 188 x 58, 238 x 58, a row per state), Default (264 x 86), Confirm (234 x 154, row 3 gray), and a grid of 18 columns of 58 x 58 cells from (450, 51), 60 x 58 apart (cell picture from (864, 376), a row per state); cell ids 0x1000 + 18 row + column. English has six rows (full-width capitals, small letters, digits, signs) and inserts the half-width letter; Chinese has seven and inserts the shown character.
  - The name starts as the default (Takurou, 拓郎), is shown from (742, 958) as GDI text (size 40, white with a black outline; a wide character moves the pen 60, a narrow one 30; the grid's rows are drawn the same way, a glyph 10 right and 8 down from its key), and holds 10 letters or 5 characters. Cells insert at the cursor, and the cursor moves only with the left and right buttons (the left and right keys move the focus, see below); the Backspace button erases before the cursor (the Backspace key too, without click sound and with the caret off), Default restores; the secondary click, Escape, Delete and Space do nothing. A click plays `ses_001.wav` only when it does something: a cell with room left, a cursor button that can move, Backspace with a letter before the cursor, Default, Confirm with a name. Confirm is gray and inert while the name is empty. A name equal to one of 142 cast names opens the `dialog2_p_<lang>.akb` notice instead (Close closes it with `ses_001.wav`, the secondary click, Escape, Delete and keypad 0 with `ses_002.wav`). The caret toggles between two bars every 300 ms.
  - Text: English typing takes only `0-9`, `A-Z` and `a-z`, always at the end of the name (the cursor goes to the end), up to 10 columns. Chinese takes the input method's committed text as full-width characters, up to 5 characters; the composition shows in the left bar from (400, 886) as black glyphs on white cells (not compared with the game). Both arrive through `Session::text_input` (and `Session::preedit` for the composition). English asks the window for no input method.
  - Ctrl+V (`Session::paste`; the player reads the clipboard, the runtime has no clipboard code): English keeps only the program's table (space, `! " # $ % & ' ( ) * , - . / 0-9 : ; > ? @ A-Z \ a-z } ~` and `× à é ö —`; no wide character); Chinese keeps everything. When the cursor plus the text's length goes past the end of the name, everything after the cursor is replaced by the text and the result cut to 10 columns, the cursor at the end. Otherwise the text overwrites in place: the whole text goes in at the cursor, replacing as many characters as fit before the fifth (none from the fifth on), and the cursor moves on by the text's length but never past 5. The 5 and the 10 are the same in both languages, as in the original. No sound.
  - Focus: one focus id, shared by the mouse and the arrow keys (the hovered item is the focused one; a mouse move changes it, a key move changes the hover). Hit areas come from `NameInput.area`, tested in file order, then the grid cells. The ten tabs (ids 0 to 9), the three kinds (0x100 to 0x102), the two scrolls (0x300, 0x301) and the bar (0x600) have no picture here but take the focus and do nothing on Enter, as in the original. From no focus Up goes to tab 0 and any other arrow to Confirm; from a tab or a scroll every arrow goes to Confirm (Right on a scroll stays); 0x600 goes nowhere. Kinds: Up to the lowest cell of column 0, 2 or 6, Down to Default, Left and Right along the three, the last one to the left button. Left, right and Backspace buttons: Up to the lowest cell of column 9, 13 or 16, Down to Confirm, Left and Right along them. Default: Up to the first kind, Right to Confirm. Confirm: Up to the Backspace button, Left to Default. A cell: Up and Down to the nearest cell with a letter in its column, Left and Right to the nearest in its row; Down from the lowest cell goes to a button by column (0: first kind, 1 to 3: second, 4 to 7: third, 8 to 11: left, 12 to 14: right, 15 to 17: Backspace). Nothing wraps, and arrows play no sound. Down from the grid's left columns lands on an invisible kind (no highlight, Enter does nothing), as the program does. Enter and keypad Enter activate the focused item as a click does; after an activation the item is drawn normal until the focus moves.
  - Storage: NUL-terminated UTF-8 at raw byte 18500 (100 bytes); the script then calls `Flag.9(100, 18500, 0)`. `:myname:`, `Game.5` and `Game.22` read the name from there (default when empty). `Flag.5(count, start, file)` reads raw bytes back from a flag file, `Flag.9` writes them; the extras' memory scenes start with `Flag.5(100, 18500, 0)`.

### Walking maps (`Game.20`, `CCps`)

The program takes a call's values from the last one back; the sub command is the last.

- Sub 0 (`name.mpx`) loads the grid: width and height (u16), then 5 bytes a 48-pixel tile, row by row; byte 4 is the wall flag.
- Sub 4 (`name.ccd`) loads the walkers. Header words: walker table, program table (a u16 offset per program), start table (camera x, y, walker x, y, a byte each), tile patterns. There are (word 1 − 9) / 14 walkers of 14 bytes: flags (1 shown, 0x10 stopped by walls, 0x20 camera follows, 0x40 hero), look, width, height, x and y (u16), facing × 16 + frame, program, command, count, program counter.
- Sub 1 places a walker from the start table, 5 gives it a program, 7 sets its flags, 14 and 22 step and turn it, 20 makes it follow another, 31 puts walkers and camera back from the raw area (camera at bytes 7097 and 7098, walker i at 7091 + 3i).
- Programs: 0 restarts, 1 and 8 face, 2 to 5 step up, down, left, right, 6 follows the trail, 9 waits, 14 takes the player's input.
- Sub 12 (`mode`, `name.eve`) loads the places: 12-byte records of id, left, top, right, bottom and the facings they take (up 1, down 2, left 4, right 8), up to 0xffff. A walker of size w × h at (x, y) is in a place when x ≤ right, x + w > left, y + 1 ≤ bottom, y + h > top and its facing is allowed. Sub 13 returns the place the hero is in.
- Sub 30 lets the player walk and returns the place reached. Arrow keys step a tile, Shift or Ctrl two; holding the mouse walks towards the pointer in eight directions. Walls stop the hero along rows y + 1 to y + h − 1 and it slides along them. A step takes 66, 33 or 16 ms by the map speed setting (`/Config/MapSpeed`: slow, normal, fast; normal 33).
- Sub 15 `(walker, x, y)` walks a walker to a target (the map jump uses it for the hero): it searches from the target towards the walker, greedily by the side the walker lies on and backing out of dead ends (walker taken as 3 × 2 tiles), then takes the lowest stamp seen along any straight line from each tile, giving a trail of directions per tile; not the shortest way. Four script bytes (1691, 1693, 1694, 4068) change the order of tries.
  - On a found trail the walker's command becomes "follow the trail" with its wall flag cleared; each tick (sub 9) it takes the step the trail gives under its feet, a diagonal as a vertical and a horizontal step.
  - Sub 17 says whether the walk goes on (written to g18 like sub 13's result, 0xffff or 0); sub 16 ends it and gives the walker back its flags, command and count. Sub 26 waits one step time between ticks.

### Faces in conversations

- The engine's `Anime` class runs the frame programs of an `.ax` file: 10 groups of 32 programs, then cells of seven numbers (kind, source x, y, width, height, screen x, y) from byte 1280, then the programs. Operations: draw a cell, wait, hold (where a program stops or pauses when asked), loop on two levels, restart, end. Each running program takes a step a frame. Cells come from the picture in sprite 5 (`Sprite.1(5, name)`) and are drawn into the background, so a cell stays until the background is loaded again; a cell of kind 3 puts the background back under itself first.
- A conversation loads its pictures first (`c_ce.mes` for one person, `irce.mes` for two): the standing picture, then the face coming in as three pictures laid onto it, then the sheet (`can.akb`, 176 x 192 faces, an expression a column) and its programs (`c.ax`, a blinking loop per expression).
- Pictures over the background are objects of `Game.13`, by id (the library only uses id 0, at 100 %): sub 0 `(frames, alpha, picture, id)` puts a screen-sized picture up (`c.akb`, a standing character), sub 1 `(frames, id, picture)` lays a same-sized picture onto an object (the faces; no object, no effect), sub 4 `(frames)` fades all objects out (library function 39, at the end of each conversation), sub 2 forgets those that faded. While an object is up, `Anime` cells are drawn onto it instead of the background. The original fades while the program goes on; here a fade is a cross-fade of the screen.
- `Game.34(index, group, name)` changes the face of a person (the group) to an expression (the program); it starts the program unless it already runs. The name is not used.
- `Anime.10` (pause at next hold) and `Anime.12` (wait for a program to end) do not wait here: the first pauses at once, the second runs the program to its end at once.
